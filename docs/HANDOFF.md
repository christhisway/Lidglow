# Lidglow handoff

This package captures Lidglow 1.0 (build 1). Open the extracted folder as a workspace and use `Sources/Lidglow/` as the source of truth. The bundled `Lidglow.app` is the compiled release. `docs/START HERE.txt` covers installation and first use.

## Current behavior

- Lidglow starts in the menu bar and opens its controls only when the icon is clicked. The panel anchors below the status item, clamps to the active display’s visible frame, and grows or shrinks smoothly from its top edge when controls appear or disappear. `GlowPanel` reports its natural height through `onGeometryChange` in the same layout pass, and `positionPanel` animates the window over the same 0.22 s as the SwiftUI fade, or without animation when Reduce Motion is on. With Reduce Motion the resize waits until SwiftUI's update finishes (coalesced to the latest height), because resizing the window inside that pass re-entered SwiftUI and left the panel the wrong height. The content is pinned to the top, both in SwiftUI and in a flipped container view, and the hosting view is kept as tall as the panel can get so SwiftUI never centers content that outgrows it. Only the bottom edge moves. It never scrolls: it's always sized to its content, and capped at the visible frame. It closes on Escape and on a click outside it. Touches on a touchscreen outside the panel reach no event monitor and don't change focus, so on macOS 27 the status item's `expandedInterfaceDelegate` lets the menu bar track the panel like a menu, and the menu bar ends the session (closing the panel) on an outside touch. Tapping the icon while the panel is open ends the session and begins a new one about 75 ms later, so a session that begins within 0.25 s of a close is cancelled. The menu bar ignores mouse clicks on the icon during a session, so the monitors close the panel on the mouse-up of a press that started while it was open. The context menu detaches the delegate while it's open, or the click would also open the panel. Control-clicking or right-clicking the icon opens a context menu with Open Settings… and Quit Lidglow. A gear in the panel header, left of the switch, and ⌘, while the panel is focused also open Settings. The Settings window has General (Open at Login), Music, and About sections. It closes with ⌘W.
- A switch in the panel's top-right corner turns Lidglow on or off. Off returns control to macOS (1.0's System mode, which migrates to the switch being off) and is the first-launch default. Modes are Standard, Breathe, and Music. Standard holds a fixed brightness, and zero brightness turns the logo off (this replaced 1.0's separate Off and Steady modes, which migrate to Standard). Breathe has Slow (8 s), Normal (5 s, the default), and Fast (3 s) speeds. Changing speed keeps the current point in the breath. Each mode has its own brightness (`brightness.Standard`, `brightness.Breathe`, `brightness.Music`, seeded once from 1.0's shared `brightness`), and the panel's slider shows the selected mode's. Only Standard can go to zero; Breathe and Music have a 5% floor (`GlowModel.minimumBrightness`), so an automatic switch to Music is never dark. The switch, selected mode, per-mode brightness, Breathe speed, Focus preset, sensitivity, idle glow, Music app settings, automatic switching, and the Music silence timeout persist.
- Music uses a private Core Audio process tap to meter playback, and detects transients per band. By default the tap includes only Spotify and Apple Music (`CATapDescription(monoMixdownOfProcesses:)`, matching each Core Audio process's bundle ID, including helpers such as `com.google.Chrome.helper`). Settings → Music has a Listen to picker: Chosen apps (the default; the checklist opens in a sheet from Choose…), All apps, or Microphone. With selected apps, `SystemMusic` listens for `kAudioHardwarePropertyProcessObjectList` changes, with no polling, and keeps no tap while none of them has audio open. The Focus preset picks the bands. Bass (the default), Mids, and Treble each use one narrow band with 24 dB/octave Butterworth filters: 40–120 Hz, 300 Hz–3 kHz, and above 6 kHz. A hit counts only when that band carries at least 15% (fully at 50%) of the whole mix's onset energy, so leaks from other instruments don't flash the logo. Full is the original wide bass, drum, and treble detector. Microphone, off by default, asks for access when chosen (`GlowModel.selectMusicSource`) and opens the default input through `AVAudioEngine` only while Music runs, rebuilding on `AVAudioEngineConfigurationChange`. Both inputs feed the same `BeatMeter` and lock-free level hand-off. Lidglow stores no recordings and has no network code.
- Settings → Music → Automatically switch to Music (off by default, shown with Chosen apps and All apps) selects Music when a chosen app, or with All apps any app, starts playing, through `select`, like a mode picked by hand. `PlaybackWatcher` reads `kAudioProcessPropertyIsRunning` (whether the process has audio running; `IsRunningOutput` never notifies listeners on macOS 27, so it only worked at launch) on the same matched process objects as the tap (`SystemMusic.processes(matching:)`), with listeners on each process and on the process list, so it needs no tap and never polls. With All apps (`bundleIDs` nil) it watches every process that plays for an app (`SystemMusic.isAppAudio`): any third-party process, any regular Apple app (`NSRunningApplication.activationPolicy == .regular`), and WebKit's media helpers. It leaves out Lidglow, whose own tap runs audio input while Music is on, and macOS's system processes, such as `systemsoundserverd` (alerts), `com.apple.MediaRemoteUIService` (volume-change feedback), Control Center, Siri, and `avconferenced` (call audio). Processes without a reverse-DNS bundle ID are system daemons and are left out too. The All apps tap uses the same rule: it's a global tap that excludes those system processes (`CATapDescription(monoGlobalTapButExcludeProcesses:)`), rebuilt from the process list listener when that set changes, so system sounds never reach the glow either. It acts only when playback starts, so a mode picked by hand while music plays stays until playback stops and starts again. Playback stopping switches back only when the timeout below is set to Automatically. A player that keeps its audio running while paused gives no new start when it resumes, so it won't switch in again until the stream closes.
- Music switches back to the mode selected before it (saved as `modeBeforeMusic`, Standard if there's none) after a stretch of silence: Settings → Music → Switch back after silence, 5, 10 (the default), 15, 30, or 60 s, or Never (`MusicTimeout`). Automatically instead switches back when `PlaybackWatcher` reports that every watched app stopped, about 2 s after a pause, and the watcher runs for it even with automatic switching into Music off. Microphone has no app stream to follow, so there it falls back to 10 s of silence (`GlowModel.silenceLimit`), and Settings says so. Silence means `music.level` stays at or below `SystemMusic.soundThreshold`, whatever the source, including while Music waits for a chosen app to open audio. The glow loop keeps the clock (`musicHeard`), so there's no extra timer, and every pause clears it, so time locked, asleep, or with the lid closed doesn't count. Leaving goes through `select`, like a mode picked by hand. An audio error keeps Music on screen so the error stays visible.
- Idle glow sets a base between hits and tracks, capped at 90% of the chosen peak so beats can still brighten the logo. The preview and physical LED both glow white.
- Each opening of the panel replays the laptop animation, then switches to a 2D logo that follows the live brightness. Reduce Motion goes directly to the 2D view.
- Lidglow steps aside for any other app that controls the logo. While it holds the logo, `LidLogo` compares each level reading with the way its last command should move the logo (up, down, or held still) over windows of at least 0.3 s, and reads the backlight mode once a second. Four unexplained moves within 20 seconds, or the backlight switched off, make it restore the logo and ask the user to quit the other app. `OtherLogoApps` also recognizes some logo apps by bundle ID (currently `co.supershort.backglow`, `app.logolight.LogoLight`, and `app.lidlight.Lidlight`): while one is running, Lidglow doesn't take control, names it in the panel, and takes over when it quits.
- Music and logo control pause on session lock, sleep, or lid closure. Normal quit restores macOS control, and so do `SIGTERM`, `SIGINT`, and `SIGHUP` (`kill`, Ctrl-C, or closing Terminal), which go through a normal quit. While Lidglow holds the logo, macOS's original backlight mode is saved in `UserDefaults` (`logoHeldOriginalMode`) and cleared by every restore. If it's still set at launch, after a crash, a force quit, or `kill -9`, `LidLogo.recoverAfterUnexpectedExit()` runs the restore sequence before anything reads the logo's state. Unsupported hardware uses the preview. So does a missing or changed CoreBrightness interface (`LogoBridge.serviceAvailable` is NO), with the status “Preview only · Lid logo service unavailable”.

| `App/Main.swift` | Entry point; starts the accessory (menu-bar) app, or only unregisters the login item for the uninstaller |
| `App/Log.swift` | `Logger` categories for local diagnostics (hardware and audio, no audio data) |
| `App/LidglowApp.swift` | Application delegate: status item and its context menu, panel window, positioning, event monitors, Settings window |
| `Glow/GlowMode.swift` | The modes and their icons, the Breathe speeds, and the Music silence timeouts |
| `Glow/GlowModel.swift` | Mode and settings state, tick loop, session lifecycle, automatic switching into and out of Music |
| `UI/GlowPanel.swift` | SwiftUI glow controls |
| `UI/GlassStyle.swift` | Shared Liquid Glass surfaces and the glass mode selector |
| `UI/LaptopPreview.swift` | SceneKit intro and live logo close-up |
| `UI/LaptopChassis.swift` | Laptop geometry and materials |
| `UI/MenuPanel.swift` | Borderless key-capable panel and its glass backdrop, clipped to its rounded shape so the window shadow follows the corners |
| `UI/SettingsWindow.swift` | The Settings window with its General (Open at Login), Music (source picker and app sheet), and About sections, built on open and released on close |
| `Audio/SystemMusic.swift` | Music sources: Core Audio tap lifecycle, per-app process matching, the process list listener, and microphone input |
| `Audio/PlaybackWatcher.swift` | Whether a chosen app is playing, from Core Audio's per-process running state, for switching to Music automatically |
| `Audio/BeatMeter.swift` | Band transient detection and the Focus (EQ) presets |
| `Hardware/LidLogo.swift` | Logo brightness transitions, original state tracking, restoration, recovery after an unexpected exit, and detection of other apps changing the logo |
| `Hardware/OtherLogoApps.swift` | Recognizes some other logo apps by bundle ID through launch and quit notifications |
| `Hardware/LaptopFinish.swift` | Automatic laptop finish detection |
| `Hardware/LogoBridge.h`, `.m` | IOKit hardware detection and dynamically loaded CoreBrightness calls, with type checks and exception guards so a changed interface reads as unavailable |
| `Resources/Info.plist` | Version, bundle ID, menu-bar activation, deployment target, and audio and microphone permission text |
| `Resources/Lidglow.entitlements` | Microphone (audio input) entitlement, applied when `build.sh` signs with a Developer ID identity and the hardened runtime |

Paths are relative to `Sources/Lidglow/`. Outside it, `Scripts/build.sh` builds and signs the app, `Scripts/package.sh` makes the release zip and its SHA-256, `Scripts/install.sh` is the Terminal installer and updater, `Scripts/uninstall.sh` removes the app, its login item, and its settings (it runs `Lidglow --unregister-login-item`, handled in `App/Main.swift`), `docs/RELEASE.md` is the release checklist, and `Scripts/test.sh` runs `Tests/BeatMeterTests/BeatMeterCheck.swift` (synthetic checks for kick, snare, hi-hat, and guitar-note response, steady audio, and silence, for every Focus preset).

## Build and verify

Run `Scripts/build.sh` from the repository root. It writes `build/Lidglow.app` and leaves the bundled release in place. Run `Scripts/test.sh` for the beat check. See [the README](../README.md) for compiler setup and Developer ID signing. No package manager, external assets, or credentials are needed for local builds. Bump both version fields in `Resources/Info.plist` for a release and keep the bundle ID stable for an in-place update.

This build was exercised on the original Mac. Previous checks confirmed that the hardware reached several brightness levels and returned to macOS control, that playback reached Music mode, and that the panel stayed within the screen when expanded. The synthetic check covers 44.1, 48, and 96 kHz. Recheck actual hardware behavior and first launch on the recipient’s Mac.

For changes, quit other logo utilities and older Lidglow copies before testing. Launch from Finder and confirm that the menu panel opens below the icon with its controls fully visible. Test Music with playback and silence, toggle Idle glow, and check restoration when the switch is turned off and on quit. A Mac without a controllable logo can exercise the interface and audio preview.

## Compatibility and distribution

The minimum is macOS 27, because every MacBook Pro with a light-up logo ships with it. It's set in `Resources/Info.plist` (`LSMinimumSystemVersion`), the `-target` in `Scripts/build.sh` and `Scripts/test.sh`, and `minimum_macos` in `Scripts/install.sh`, so keep them in step. With that floor, the panel always uses the system Liquid Glass (`GlassStyle.swift`, `MenuPanel.swift`) and needs no material fallback. Letting older macOS open an inert, unsupported panel is tracked post-beta in #61. Logo control uses a private CoreBrightness interface and may need adjustment after macOS updates. Keep hardware detection and restoration intact when changing that path. Core Audio capture uses the public process tap API and needs per-app system audio permission.

Releases are arm64 and unnotarized on purpose (no paid Developer ID). They're installed with `Scripts/install.sh`, which downloads with `curl` so no quarantine attribute is set, and verifies the published SHA-256. Sign releases with the owner's self-signed `Lidglow Self-Signed` identity (`LIDGLOW_SIGN_IDENTITY`) so the audio permission survives updates. Open at Login uses `SMAppService.mainApp`. The steps below only apply if a Developer ID is ever used. For a release that can pass Gatekeeper’s standard distribution checks, use a Developer ID Application signature and Apple notarization. After notarization, staple the ticket to the app and then recreate the ZIP. [Apple’s notarization workflow](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/customizing-the-notarization-workflow)
